<h2>What is MonkiiBoard?</h2>

<p>
MonkiiBoard is a project initialized in 2024, where I founded wanting to create open-sourced computer peripherals
for low-income communities, along with DIY STEM individuals who wants to build things their own. MonkiiBoard is
currently solely developing keyboards.
</p>

<p>
Keyboards, custom PCBs, microcontrollers, FPGAs, firmware, games, weird little
hardware projects and whatever else I happen to get curious about.
</p>

<h3>MonkiiPad20</h3>

<p>
A 20 key macropad powered by an RP2040 Zero, arranged as a 4 by 6 diode matrix.
It has an SSD1306 OLED running over a second I2C bus, two switchable layers
called <b>Shortcuter</b> and <b>Typist</b>, and a little robot eye that watches
you when the board is left alone.
</p>

<h3>MonkiiBoard39</h3>

<p>
A 39 key board built around a Pro Micro and ATmega32U4.
It uses a 4 by 10 matrix, sticky modifiers for easier one handed shortcuts,
and a double tap Windows key because sometimes one press just isn't enough.
</p>

<h3>MonkiiPad3x3</h3>

<p>
Nine keys. Three by three. That's it.
Hold one key for a moment and the entire pad transforms from a numpad into a
macro layer packed with arrows and editing shortcuts.
</p>

<h3>MonkiiPad20 PCB</h3>

<p>
My first proper PCB designed in KiCad.
A two layer, 120 by 86 mm board with a diode footprint for every switch and a
clearly labelled RP2040 Zero header, packaged up into one clean Gerber set.
</p>

<h3>Zombie Survival</h3>

<p>
A tiny survival game written in Verilog and running directly on a Tang Nano 4K FPGA.
Physical buttons become the controls and the hardware becomes the game console.
Because apparently making a game wasn't difficult enough without making the
computer too.
</p>
